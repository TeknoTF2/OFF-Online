# OFF TTRPG — Economy Document

Companion to the System Document and Campaign Bestiary. This document defines the item catalog, equipment, currency, and rest structure. Its spine is the attrition budget: at the chosen difficulty, the party fights **7–8 encounters per rest zone**, and everything below is sized against that number.

GM rulings baked in: survival-horror attrition (7–8 fights/leg), full equipment lines, Abaddon's Meat as a rare drop capped at one held.

## Part 1 — The dial and the pacing

A **leg** is the stretch between rest zones: 7–8 standard fights plus, on boss legs, the boss. One leg is almost exactly one session of play — **rest zones are session boundaries**, and the GM should treat them that way deliberately: the party limps in, spends, restocks, and the session ends on the exhale.

Rest zones restore all HP and CP and cure all statuses. There is no other free recovery anywhere in the system, which is what makes the numbers below matter.

**Placement:** two rest zones per zone (mid-zone and pre-boss), giving each zone two legs. The purified zones run one leg each (three total), the Room runs two, and the finale is fought off the Room's second rest. Campaign total: roughly thirteen legs — thirteen to fifteen sessions of combat play.

## Part 2 — The attrition budget (computed from the bestiary)

Model: the party heals ~45% of incoming damage in combat via competences (tempo healing), covers the rest with items between fights, and rides ~30% of its HP pool as an accepted buffer. Damage competences run in ~60% of fights at ~3 casts each. Drain enemies cost ~8 CP per fight on average where present.

| Leg (at level) | Damage taken / leg | Heal casts (CP) | Total CP burn | Party CP pool | **CP item gap** | **HP item gap** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 (~3) | ~2,300 | 21 (374) | ~680 | 400 | **~280** | **~1,050** |
| Zone 2 (~8) | ~4,300 | 28 (501) | ~800 | 560 | **~245** | **~2,050** |
| Zone 3 (~13) | ~8,500 | 32 (699) | ~1,000 | 730 | **~270** | **~4,200** |
| The Room (~17) | ~11,600 | 39 (848) | ~1,150 | 860 | **~290** | **~5,900** |

Two structural facts fall out. **The CP gap is flat** — roughly 250–290 for the whole campaign, because CP pools grow linearly alongside burn. CP items are therefore a constant-size purchase forever: about five Silver Flesh or two Golden per leg. **The HP gap is the survival-horror axis** — it scales 6× across the campaign, and HP items, not competence healing, are what carry the party through a leg. The Purifier's pool is six heals; the leg needs twenty to forty. Counting tickets is the horror, by design.

Purified-zone legs run hotter: superboss legs should budget +30% on both gaps, and the party should feel that in the shop prices there.

## Part 3 — The item catalog

Canon names and tiers preserved; numbers restatted against the budget. Prices are Zone 1 baseline — see Part 6 for scaling.

| Item | Price (Z1) | Effect | Per-leg usage guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luck Ticket** | 40 | Restores 120 HP to one character | Zone 1–2 workhorse: 8–9/leg early |
| **Fortune Ticket** | 240 | Restores 600 HP to one character | Zone 2 onward: 3–10/leg, the mid/late workhorse |
| **Silver Flesh** | 180 | Restores 50 CP | ~5/leg, all campaign |
| **Golden Flesh** | 480 | Restores 100 CP | The bulk option; 2–3 replace 5 Silver |
| **Joker** | 150 | Revives at **35% max HP**, 50% gauge (system doc overrides canon's 25%) | 2/leg early, 3–4 in the Room and beyond |
| **Moloch's Meat** | 30 | Cures Poisoned | Cheap insurance from Zone 3 on |
| **Belial's Meat** | 100 | Cures all statuses on one character | 2–3/leg from Zone 2 on; the answer to Muted supports |
| **Eye** | 20 | Wide Angle in a bottle | Situational; boss-critical vs Maldicion and the Gnosticī |
| **Inspiration** | 200 | Attack item: 75 base damage + ATK + ESP scaling, no CP | CP-free turns for support classes |
| **Expiration** | 900 | Attack item: 400 base + scaling | The emergency nuke; superboss races |
| **Abaddon's Meat** | not sold | Full party HP+CP restore, all statuses cured. **Out of combat only. Rare drop. Party may hold ONE.** | The parachute — see below |

**Abaddon's rule.** One held, ever; a second drop is refused (convert to a Fortune Ticket). It drops only from elites and superbosses at low rates, and using it mid-leg is the panic button that saves a doomed leg exactly once. This is the pressure valve that makes 7–8 fights survivable without making rest zones optional.

**Item heals versus the Purifier.** A Fortune Ticket (600 HP, costs a turn and money) out-heals Save Second Base (~150, costs a turn and 22 CP). This is intended and correct at this difficulty: items are the between-fights and emergency economy, competence healing is the *in-tempo* economy — the Purifier heals mid-fight because CP is on hand when credits aren't, and because item supply is finite per leg. If playtest shows the Purifier feeling obsolete, cut Fortune Ticket to 400 before touching anything else.

**Status cures and Cob.** Belial's Meat is the item answer to statuses; Cob's Advent locks items *and* competences precisely because both answers exist. Nothing needs adjusting — just know the fight was designed against this catalog.

## Part 4 — Equipment (full lines, reconciled with the curves)

The system document's stat checkpoints budget no room for gear, so equipment is not additive — **the printed checkpoint tables assume tier-appropriate equipment**. Roughly 15% of every checkpoint stat is gear share. A character wearing the current zone's tier is *on curve* — exactly the bestiary math. A character a tier behind is measurably below curve, and the shop is how you stay alive.

**Slots:** two per character — a weapon-analog and a charm. **Tiers:** T1 through T4, one per zone, sold by Zacharie in that zone; **T5** pieces exist only as superboss drops and grant the campaign's only over-curve edge (+5%).

Each tier piece carries that class's gear share of the zone's stat growth, split weapon (ATK or ESP, per class scaling) / charm (HP, DEF, or the stat the class leans on). Canon pieces slot in by name where they exist: the Batter's bat line for the Purifier, and the canon add-on gear — Left/Right Glove (+ATK), Starred Shorts and Nerves of Steel (+DEF), Lucky Coin (+ESP/+AGI) — distributed to Alpha, Omega, and Epsilon. **The Bandit and the Burnt need invented lines** (they have no canon counterpart): suggested flavor, star charts and lockpicks for the Bandit, dessert molds and oven mitts for the Burnt. AGI on gear follows the system doc's rarity rule: it appears on exactly one charm line (Lucky Coin's), nowhere else.

Pricing: a full tier for one character costs roughly 600 credits at Zone 1 scale, rising per zone. Gearing six characters is the single largest expense per zone — that is deliberate; it is what the boss-leg income is for.

## Part 5 — Orbs (permanent growth)

Canon's permanent stat items, capped hard because six players with a shared wallet would otherwise snowball: **Zacharie stocks exactly one of each orb per zone** (Taurus +50 max HP, Libra +15 max CP, Scorpio +5 ATK, Gemini +5 DEF, Capricorn +5 ESP), priced at 300 × zone number. **Virgo (+5 AGI) appears exactly twice in the campaign** — once in Zone 3, once in the purified zones — honoring AGI as the rarest stat in the game. Superbosses may drop one bonus orb each. Who gets each orb is a party decision, and arguing about it is content.

## Part 6 — Income, prices, and Zacharie

Income is designed top-down: a leg's credit income should cover its consumable basket with partial progress toward the zone's gear tier, and the boss leg's payout closes the gear gap. Canon drop credits are discarded (they were tuned for an economy where money didn't matter); set per-fight awards as follows, split nothing — the wallet is shared like the inventory.

| Zone | Credits / standard fight | Boss payout | Leg income (~7.5 fights) | Consumable basket / leg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 | 220 | 2,500 | ~1,650 | ~1,400 |
| Zone 2 | 420 | 5,000 | ~3,150 | ~2,600 |
| Zone 3 | 700 | 8,000 | ~5,250 | ~4,300 |
| The Room | 950 | 12,000 | ~7,100 | ~5,800 |
| Purified | 1,300 | superboss 15,000 | ~9,750 | ~7,500 (+30% rule) |

Prices scale with the tickets: Fortune Tickets and Flesh cost 1× in Zone 1 terms, 1.5× in Zone 3, 2× in the Room and purified zones. The margins above are deliberately thin — a party that skips fights shops short, and a party that farms extends sessions to buy slack. Both are the dial working.

**Zacharie's stock per visit:** current gear tier (all classes), tickets and Flesh in leg quantities plus ~30% slack, 3 Jokers, cures, 2 Inspirations, the zone's orbs, and — from Zone 2 on — one Expiration. He appears at every rest zone, because he always does.

## Part 7 — The Bandit's economy

The class that generates supply. **Cutpurse** (+1 drop on Bandit kills): at a ~25% kill share, four to five bonus drops per leg, drawn from the zone's consumable table. **Light Fingers** (5% chance any used item isn't consumed): at ~14 item uses per leg, roughly 0.7 free items — quietly, a ~5% discount on the entire party economy, forever. **Ursa Shot** (16 CP, steal): steals from the zone steal table; worth casting when the expected item beats 16 CP of value — from Zone 2 on, enemy steal tables should include Silver Flesh (break-even) with rare Fortune Tickets and, on elites, Inspirations (clear profit). Superbosses each carry one unique steal — the only way to obtain a second-copy T5 accessory, which makes Ursa Shot a turn worth spending in the endgame.

A party without its Bandit for a session runs ~10% poorer. That is the class contribution, priced.

## Part 8 — Interactions ledger

Enemy CP drains (Tiburce, Massu, 笑, Fool's Mate, Dramatic Crescendo) now have a price: every point drained is ~3.6 credits of Silver Flesh — a Massu pack that lands three drains has stolen a Luck Ticket. Sugar's Dummies and Source's Flourish burn the party's *item* clock, not just fight time, which is why those fights sit at the end of legs. And the finale's Phase 2 arrives after the Room's last leg has already spent the party down — the Bad Batter fight is balanced assuming roughly one leg's slack of items remains, no more. Stock accordingly, or die accordingly.
